# The video — script, 3:00 hard cap

One narrated take over screen recording, edited. ~420 spoken words. Every line is written to be
said, not read — if a sentence fights your mouth, change it, keep the beat.

## Pre-flight (do all of these, then record)

- Relay freshly reseeded; Waggle tab hard-reloaded; no leftover cards in the dock.
- Waggle in ChatGPT desktop → **Work** → built-in browser. Header: **WebMCP · 12 tools** green,
  **Rule from Buzz · on**, owner key in both Waggle and Buzz desktop.
- Buzz desktop on `#general`, window beside ChatGPT. Phone with Buzz on `#waggle-drafts` if it runs.
- Rehearse Scene 2 twice; ChatGPT's tool discovery is slow — you'll cut that dead air.

## Script

**[0:00 — Title card: 🐝 · Waggle · "Your agent can dance. Only you can fly."]**

> Waggle. Your agent can dance. Only you can fly.

**[0:05 — Slide: two columns. "Agent in the room — own key" / "Agent at your elbow — NO key"]**

> When we put agents in chat, we give them accounts. Their own key, their own audit trail. That's
> right for an agent that's a teammate. It's wrong for the agent at your elbow — the one reading
> over your shoulder. That agent should have no key at all. WebMCP finally makes it buildable:
> the tools live in my tab, under my session. Nothing hosted. Nothing pretending to be me.

**[0:30 — ChatGPT Work, Waggle open. Click **Site tools** in the address bar; the 12 tools list.]**

> This is Waggle — a chat client on a relay I own. The page registers twelve tools on
> document-dot-modelContext. Seven read. Five propose. Zero can send.

**[0:42 — Split screen: Buzz desktop on #general (Marco's question visible) | ChatGPT+Waggle.]**

> Here's my team in Buzz. Marco wants to know if the shop has a leather bag under two hundred
> dollars. Watch what I don't do: I don't look it up.

**[0:52 — Type to ChatGPT: "Check the shop for a leather bag under $200 and answer Marco." —
CUT the discovery lag; resume as it browses the storefront, then Waggle.]**

> The agent finds the store in the thread, uses the storefront's own WebMCP tools to search it —
> that's Vercel's shop — then comes back to Waggle and calls propose underscore reply.
> Two sites. One errand. And still — nothing has been sent.

**[1:15 — Buzz, #waggle-drafts: the 🐝 draft post with bag + price. Phone shot if you have it.]**

> It proposed. Into my private drafts room, on my relay. I'm not even in Waggle anymore — this is
> Buzz, on my phone.

**[1:24 — Tap ✅. Cut to #general: the reply lands in Marco's thread, 🐝 badge by your name.]**

> One reaction. That reaction is a signed event — by me. The tab holding my key sees it and signs
> the real reply. And Buzz shows the bee: everyone can see an agent drafted it, and I signed it.

**[1:40 — Second draft in #waggle-drafts. Reply to it in Buzz with better wording; it lands.]**

> Don't like the words? Reply with better ones. My edit becomes the message — and the diff between
> the agent's draft and mine is a correction log I own.

**[1:52 — Waggle: click Hold the room → "back at 5:00". Buzz shows the 🐝 status. Say to ChatGPT:
"Handle what's waiting on me." Cards stack — speed this up in the edit.]**

> Stepping out? Tell the room. My status says the agent is drafting and when I rule. One sentence —
> handle what's waiting on me — and everything unanswered becomes a draft. I judge the stack from
> my phone. Composing is the agent's job. Judging is mine.

**[2:12 — Event JSON on screen, `client: waggle` and `proposed-by: webmcp` highlighted.]**

> On the wire, every signed event says whether an agent drafted it. That tag is the audit trail.

**[2:20 — Quick clip: Claude-in-Chrome typing into the composer, message appears unmarked.]**

> One honest caveat. A screenshot-and-click agent can still drive my cursor and pretend to be me —
> no page can stop that. That's exactly the case for WebMCP: a door for agents that isn't
> pretending to be the human.

**[2:35 — Slide: WaggleContext seam → any client · drafts = plain NIP-29 group · rulings = plain
reactions · "Buzz needed zero changes"]**

> Buzz needed zero changes to be the gavel — the drafts are a private group, the rulings are plain
> reactions. The tools module drops into any client. Approval gates, without a workflow engine.

**[2:48 — End card: app.waggle.thecrowbarcrew.cc · github.com/txmyer-dev/waggle]**

> Waggle. The agent dances. You decide whether to fly.

**[2:55 — hold the card to 3:00]**
